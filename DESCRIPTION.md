Install Outfitter and you have a shop. Not a theme to find, install and learn — the store itself, rendering its own pages, dark and finished before you touch anything, selling what is already in your catalog.

Then you open the editor.

Click the headline on your own home page and type over it. Drag the testimonials above the product grid. Hide the announcement bar until the sale starts. Flip the preview to Tablet, then Mobile, and watch that row reflow. Seven colors draw the whole store — buttons, prices, hairlines, the backdrops behind product photos.

Home, product and category pages are each a stack of sections — seventeen types:

- **The goods.** One product large beside your copy, a product row, a grid of the newest or one category, category tiles.
- **The company.** A hero, a picture beside a paragraph, a how-it-works in three steps.
- **The proof.** Customer quotes, an FAQ, big figures with a label under each, a logo strip for the teams that wear you.
- **The nudge.** An announcement bar for a shipping threshold, a call to action at the end.

Arrange them around the part that has to stay: the product page keeps its product, the category page its products. Neither can be deleted, so marketing goes above and below. Empty the home list and the standard hero and product grid come back, not a blank page.

Every line of the store's own copy is yours in every language you sell in — 139 translatable strings, down to the cart drawer's empty message and the checkout labels. Add a logo, set how long a page stays cached at the edge, and that is most of the settings panel.

Cart, checkout and account are laid out in code and plain on purpose; Outfitter adds nothing to configure between add to cart and the receipt. Rates and tracking come off ordinary Swell records, so any fulfillment app works here — carrier and tracking reach the shopper's order history once a shipment is recorded. Starting from nothing, Printful pairs well: nothing is made until someone orders it, so there is no stock to buy.

Built for a small company that wants a merch store, not a theme project — someone who would rather write copy than shop for themes. Setup is settings and clicks. And for whoever inherits it: this storefront renders its own markup, so everything past the settings is yours.
