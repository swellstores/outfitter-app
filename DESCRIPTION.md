Outfitter is a storefront for selling company merch — tees, hoodies, caps, mugs, totes — that you run yourself, from the dashboard, without a theme and without a developer on call.

**Build the pages from sections.** Home, product and category are each a stack you arrange in the editor: drag a row to reorder it, click any headline on your own page and type over it, hide the announcement bar until the sale starts, flip the preview to Tablet or Mobile to see the row reflow. Seventeen section types; the ones you will reach for most —

- **Show the goods.** A product grid, a product row, one product large beside your copy, category tiles.
- **Say who you are.** A hero, an image beside a paragraph, a how-it-works in three steps.
- **Give them a reason.** Customer quotes, an FAQ, figures with a label under each, a logo strip.
- **Ask for the order.** An announcement bar for a shipping threshold, a call to action at the end.

The product page always keeps its product and the category page its products, so no arrangement you save can leave a page with nothing to sell. Empty the home list and the standard hero and grid come back rather than a blank screen.

**Sell the variations you actually stock.** Size and color pick the variant; a color can carry its own photograph, so choosing one swaps the product shot. Options that are not variants work too — a gift note, a thread color, a checkbox that adds a surcharge — and each one follows the line through the cart to the order, priced as you set it.

**Brand it in the settings panel.** A store name, a logo, seven colors that draw everything from buttons to the hairlines between rows, and how long a page is cached at the edge. Every line of the store's own copy is yours in each language you sell in — 143 strings, down to the cart drawer's empty message — so a second language needs no plugin.

**Fulfill it however you like.** Cart and account pages are plain on purpose, and checkout is a one-screen review — line items, shipping services, the total — that hands off to Swell's hosted checkout. Outfitter never touches payment. Shipping rates and tracking come from ordinary Swell records, so any fulfillment app works, and carrier and tracking reach the shopper's order history as soon as a shipment is recorded. Print on demand suits a merch store starting from nothing — nothing is made until someone orders it — and the Printful app handles it, but nothing here requires it.

**Show it before it sells anything.** Turn on demo mode and every page says plainly that orders are not fulfilled, so you can put the store in front of your team while the catalog is still a draft.

And because it renders its own markup rather than running a theme, a developer who inherits it can change anything past the settings.
